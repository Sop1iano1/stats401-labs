Lab 6 — GDP Hierarchy

Comparing two treemap tiling strategies for the same continent → area → country hierarchy.

How to read the treemaps

Each rectangle represents a country. Rectangle area encodes GDP, so larger rectangles correspond to larger GDP values. Color encodes GDP status. Hover over a country for detailed information.

Some countries have relatively small GDP values and therefore appear as very small rectangles in the overall view. In the second treemap, you can use the mouse wheel to zoom in and drag the visualization to explore smaller regions. Hover over a country after zooming to view its detailed information.

GDP status Increase Unchanged Decrease

Treemap 1 — Squarify

Uses d3.treemapSquarify to favor more compact rectangles.

Treemap 2 — Slice & Dice

Uses d3.treemapSliceDice to alternate subdivision direction by depth.

Interactive: Scroll to zoom in or out, drag to move around the treemap, and hover over a country to see its detailed GDP information. Zooming makes smaller countries and regions easier to inspect.

Design Description

I use rectangle area to encode GDP because GDP is quantitative and the part-to-whole structure of a treemap makes large differences visible at a glance. Color encodes GDP status because Increase, Unchanged, and Decrease are categorical states; a consistent three-category legend makes the comparison between the two treemaps straightforward.

I keep the same hierarchy, data values, colors, labels, and tooltip behavior in both views, changing only the tiling strategy. The Squarify layout generally produces more compact rectangles, which makes country labels and individual regions easier to inspect when rectangle sizes vary substantially. Slice-and-Dice produces more directional and elongated partitions, making hierarchical grouping and the depth structure more visually apparent but sometimes making individual countries harder to compare.

Because some countries have relatively small GDP values, their rectangles can be difficult to inspect in the full Slice-and-Dice view. The zoom and pan interaction allows users to explore these smaller regions in greater detail while preserving the original GDP-based area encoding.
