Analysis

The original dataset contains 30,427 financial tweet records with tweet text and market-related attributes. I cleaned the full dataset using Python and pandas by checking missing values, exact duplicates, timestamps, numeric fields, categorical variables, and text formatting. For text analysis, the tweet text was normalized, tokenized, filtered for stop words, and lemmatized before constructing the DTM and TF-IDF representations. For sentiment analysis, I used a reproducible random sample of 3,000 tweets (random state = 401). The original text was only lightly normalized before being processed with the cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest model. Each tweet was assigned the sentiment with the highest predicted probability, and the sentiment score was calculated as positive probability minus negative probability.

The 3,000-tweet sentiment sample is predominantly neutral, with 2,227 tweets classified as Neutral, compared with 654 Positive and 119 Negative tweets. The visualization further compares sentiment composition across four market regimes. Volatile periods have the highest negative sentiment share (5.7%) and the lowest positive share (20.5%), while also having the lowest average sentiment score (0.220). In contrast, trending-down periods have the lowest negative share (2.2%), the highest positive share (24.6%), and the highest average sentiment score (0.292). These results suggest an association between market regime and the distribution of tweet sentiment in this sample.
